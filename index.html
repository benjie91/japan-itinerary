The route, day by day

Read it like a rail line: each stop is a base city, days branch off it, and the links between are the train or bus. Ember stations are where the foliage should be at its best.

Osaka

Days 1–3 · base · Tenmabashi
D1 Sat 14 Nov

Arrive · Osaka Castle & Pokémon

08:35 land KIX · 10:30 hotel, bags · 11:30 lunch · 12:30 Osaka Castle Park · 14:00 Namba Yasaka Shrine · 15:00 Den Den Town + DX · 17:30 Dōtonbori dinner

An easy, low-transit first day. Osaka is only an hour ahead of Singapore, so it's red-eye tiredness rather than real jet lag — drop bags and ease into the city from the castle down to Dōtonbori.

  1. Land KIX · 08:35 — grab an IC card and head in. Cheapest is the Nankai Airport Express to Namba (~¥930, no reservation), then the Osaka Metro to Tenmabashi (~¥240) — about ~¥1,170 all-in, ~1 h with a transfer. (The faster reserved Rapi:t is ~¥1,490, so with the subway ~¥1,730.) If time permits, the no-transfer alternative is the Airport Limousine Bus (KATE, stop 10): the 10:10 departure runs straight to Temmabashi Station — your hotel’s doorstep — arriving ~11:15, ~¥1,800 one-way (a little less prebooked). About ¥600 more than the Airport Express route — on par with the Rapi:t — but one seat, no stairs; just slightly slower, nudging lunch later.
  2. Hotel, drop bags · 10:30 — check-in is later, so leave luggage at Hotel Keihan Temmabashi Ekimae and head straight out.
  3. Lunch · 11:30 — fried rice and noodles (焼飯と麺), a 1-min walk from Keihan Tenmabashi on the way to the castle: fragrant fried rice and clear chuka soba, cash-only ticket machine downstairs, seats upstairs. No reservations — go near the 11:00 open to beat the queue, and note it can sell out.
  4. Osaka Castle Park · 12:30 — a short walk on: lawns, the 300-year-old giant ginkgo and the momiji, an easy first-day autumn view.
  5. Namba Yasaka Shrine · 14:00 — subway south to Namba for the dramatic giant lion-head stage (Ema-den), a quick, photogenic stop ~5 min from the station — then work north through Minami.
  6. Den Den Town + DX Pokémon · 15:00 — Nipponbashi’s electronics & anime strip, then north to the DX Pokémon Center and Pokémon Café at Daimaru Shinsaibashi (9F, open to 20:00).
  7. Dōtonbori dinner · 17:30 — the Glico sign, Ebisubashi and the canal for a first street-food dinner, then the short hop north to the hotel. Low on energy? Keep it to the castle park and an early dinner near the hotel.
Autumn
Mid-Nov is early for central-Osaka kōyō (the city peaks late Nov, later still in 2026), so today is the season just starting — the Osaka Castle ginkgo and Nakanoshima roses turning, the Midōsuji ginkgo going gold. Your real foliage begins at Minoh tomorrow.
Pokémon
Pokémon Center Osaka DX — Daimaru Shinsaibashi 9F, a short walk from Dōtonbori. The Pokémon Café is next door (reservation-only — book the moment slots open). Doing DX now keeps the flagship Umeda store as the D14 highlight.
Eat
Lunch is fried rice and noodles (焼飯と麺) by Tenmabashi — walk-in only, English/Chinese/Korean menus, roughly ¥1,000–1,500 for a ramen-and-fried-rice set. Dinner in Dōtonbori: Namba Okonomiyaki Ajinoya Honten (reserve or queue) plus takoyaki from the stalls; 551 Horai pork buns, or mochi-pounding at Oh Mochi.
D2 Sun 15 Nov

Katsuō-ji & Minoh Falls

09:00 depart · 10:00 Katsuō-ji · 12:30 Minoh Falls · 14:30 Umeda lunch + Pokémon · 16:00 Keitakuen · 18:00 Shinsekai dinner

A fuller foliage day in the Minoh hills, best done top-down — mountain temple first, then walk down to the falls.

  1. Depart · 09:00 — Midōsuji Line to its terminus Minoh-Kayano, then Hankyu Bus #30 to Katsuō-ji (~20 min · ¥800).
  2. Katsuō-ji · 10:00 — the 1,300-year-old mountain daruma temple, terraces strewn with thousands of little red dolls and wrapped in maples around a red-bridged pond; colours a touch earlier up here.
  3. Walk down · 11:30 — the forest trail toward the falls (~40 min, mostly downhill), or a taxi to skip the hilly middle. Last bus back from the temple is ~15:00, which is exactly why you walk.
  4. Minoh Falls · 12:30 — the 33 m falls, then the paved 2.7 km Taki-michi past momiji-tempura stalls to Hankyu Minoo Station.
  5. Back to Umeda · 14:00 — the Hankyu from Minoo terminates right at Osaka-Umeda (~25 min), steps from the Pokémon Center.
  6. Umeda · lunch + Pokémon · 14:30 — lunch in the LUCUA 1100 / Daimaru food halls, then Pokémon Center Osaka on Daimaru Umeda’s 13F (open to 20:00), with Nintendo Osaka on the same floor.
  7. Keitakuen · 16:00 — Midōsuji south to Tennoji for the quiet pond-and-maple garden in Tennoji Park (open to 17:00), on the way to dinner.
  8. Shinsekai · 18:00 — Tsūtenkaku and a retro-Osaka dinner next door. Too tired? Café-hop the machiya cafés of Nakazakichō, or the riverside terraces around Kitahama near your hotel.
Transit
Midōsuji Line to its terminus Minoh-Kayano (Tanimachi to Umeda first), then Hankyu Bus #30 from stand 8 to Katsuō-ji (20 min · ¥800 · every 20–30 min on a Sunday). Go early: the last bus back from the temple leaves around 3 pm, which is exactly why you walk down to the falls instead. Exit via the Taki-michi to Hankyu Minoo Station. Temple ¥500; the falls trail occasionally closes after storms, so check status.
Eat
On the Minoh path: momiji tempura — candied deep-fried maple leaves — from Koyo-ryorikan or Momotaro. Lunch up at Umeda — the LUCUA 1100 and Daimaru “Umekita” food floors, or Hankyu Sanbangai’s restaurant warren under the station. Dinner: Kushikatsu Daruma, the original skewer joint in Shinsekai. Sweet stop: Torori warabi mochi and a matcha latte in Tenjinbashi.
D3 Mon 16 Nov

Amanohashidate, Ine & Miyama · Kyoto by the Sea

07:05 meet Nihonbashi · 09:50 Ine boathouses · 11:30 Amanohashidate + cable car · 15:00 Miyama village · ~17:40 Kitahama riverside

A booked full-day coach up to the Sea of Japan coast — “Kyoto by the Sea” — pairing one of Japan’s Three Views with two storybook villages. A long day (~10 hrs) and a change of pace from the temples, but it bundles the awkward transfers that make this corner impossible to do well by public transport. It disbands in Shinsaibashi, so your evening sorts itself.

  1. Meet at Nihonbashi · 07:05 — Exit 2 (look for the yellow “GOGODAY” flag); 7:20 departure. ~15 min by subway from Tenmabashi, so an early start — leave the hotel ~6:40 and be punctual, shared tours don’t wait.
  2. Ine no Funaya · 09:50 — the “Venice of Japan,” ~230 wooden boathouses built out over the bay; a boat cruise loops the harbour, seagulls swooping in for the crackers you’re handed.
  3. Amanohashidate · 11:30 — one of Japan’s Three Views, a 3.6 km pine sandbar; ride the cable car up to Viewland for the “Flying Dragon” panorama (the tradition is to view it upside-down between your legs). ~2 hrs, lunch at your own expense.
  4. Miyama thatched village · 15:00 — the kayabuki-no-sato: a cluster of Edo-era thatched-roof farmhouses in a quiet valley, like stepping into an old woodblock print (~40 min).
  5. Kitahama & Nakanoshima · from ~17:40 — from the Shinsaibashi drop-off, head north to the Ōkawa riverside (a change from D1’s Minami): the leafy island, the Central Public Hall lit up, and a riverside dinner on the Kitahama terraces — a short walk from your hotel to wind down after the long day.
Transit
Booked coach tour (Pelago), English/Chinese/Japanese guide, ~10 hrs. Meets Nihonbashi Exit 2 · 07:05 (7:20 departure) and disbands at Shinsaibashi ~17:40. Pick the package with the cable car + Ine boat included. One free bag per person (extra suitcase ¥2,000).
Notes
One of the rare Kansai trips where a tour beats DIY — Ine has no railway and buses run only about hourly, so doing it yourself is long, costly and fragile. Sea-of-Japan weather is changeable in late Nov; the operator cancels for typhoon/snow (notified by 6 pm the day before), and the fare is non-refundable close in. It’s an early 7:05 start, so an easy night beforehand helps.
Eat
Lunch is at your own expense — seafood around Amanohashidate and Chion-ji, and Ine’s bayside cafés and seafood. Dinner on the Kitahama riverside terraces (Ōkawa views, near the hotel) to wind down — or grab it at the Shinsaibashi drop-off if you’d rather not move.

Kyoto

Days 4–6 · foliage peak
D4 Tue 17 Nov

Kyoto & Uji · aqueduct walk

08:00 check out · 09:00 hotel, drop bags · 09:45 Uji + matcha ramen · 14:00 Nanzen-ji + Eikan-dō · 16:30 Dragon Burger dinner · 17:30 Kōdai-ji light-up · 19:00 Gion → Pontochō stroll

Bags down first, then Uji. Check out around 8:00, ride into Kyoto Station (~45 min) and drop your bags at the hotel two minutes from the Hachijo exit — no lockers to chase. Then hop the JR Nara line south to Uji (~20 min), reaching the matcha town by ~9:45. Byōdō-in’s Phoenix Hall sits mirrored in its pond with maples around the rim, and the Omotesandō approach is lined with centuries-old tea houses. Back on the JR line by early afternoon and up to Higashiyama, pausing at Murin-an, a Meiji-era garden of maples and streams with the mountains borrowed as backdrop (reserve online; peak-autumn ~¥1,500), before the afternoon walk — Nanzen-ji and the maples of Eikan-dō by day, then Kōdai-ji’s evening light-up (Eikan-dō’s own illumination doesn’t begin until Nov 20).

  1. Kyoto Station · 09:00 — drop your bags at the hotel, two minutes from the Hachijo East exit, then straight back to the platform. No lockers to chase.
  2. Uji · 09:45 — JR Nara line down (~20 min): Byōdō-in (Phoenix Hall mirrored in its pond, maples round the rim; ¥700 grounds), then lunch on the Omotesandō — go for the gimmick, matcha ramen (green-tea noodles + matcha gyoza) at Tanakakusho, or classic cha-soba (matcha soba) at Torikiku; chase it with a matcha soft-serve, and ten minutes at the Uji Bridge.
  3. Keage · 14:00 — JR back up to Kyoto and the subway Tōzai Line to Keage; the old railway slope (Murin-an garden is 7 min away if you want it first).
  4. Nanzen-ji — free grounds and the red-brick Suirokaku aqueduct in daylight; paid halls close ~16:30.
  5. Eikan-dō · by day — right next door, “Eikan-dō of the maples”: about 3,000 of them around Hōjō Pond. The night light-up doesn’t start until Nov 20, but the daytime visit is open (in by ~16:30, gates 17:00, ¥1,000). Philosopher’s Path begins just past here for a short stretch.
  6. Dragon Burger 南禅寺店 · dinner ~16:30 — right after Eikan-dō, a short walk to Keage for juicy wagyu burgers (yuzu, shiso, wasabi; ~¥1,800), 4.7-rated — an early dinner also beats the queue. (Closed Weds — fine, you’re here Tuesday.)
  7. Kōdai-ji light-up · from 17:30 — bus south (~15–20 min) to Higashiyama’s Kōdai-ji for the finale: projection-mapping illumination and the pond mirroring the maples (open from Nov 15, to ~22:00).
  8. Gion → Pontochō stroll · ~19:00 — walk it off: down past Yasaka Shrine, along Gion’s Hanamikoji and the Shirakawa canal, across Shijō to Pontochō’s lantern-lit riverside alley for a nightcap or dessert (you’ve already eaten). ~25–30 min; stick to the main lanes — Gion’s small private alleys are off-limits to tourists. Then Keihan/subway back to the hotel.
Transit
Check out ~8:00. Into Kyoto Station ~45 min (Keihan to Tofukuji, one stop on the JR Nara line; or JR from Osaka Station) — drop bags at the hotel, no lockers needed. Then JR Nara line down to JR Uji (~20 min) and back. For the afternoon, JR to Kyoto and subway Tōzai Line to Keage. Begin the walk by 2 pm so Nanzen-ji, the aqueduct and Eikan-dō’s maples catch daylight (Eikan-dō gates close 17:00); then a short bus south to Kōdai-ji for the 17:00 light-up. Confirm dates on the temples’ sites.
Eat
Uji is matcha central — a matcha ramen lunch (green-tea noodles + matcha gyoza) at Tanakakusho, or classic cha-soba at Torikiku, plus a matcha soft-serve or parfait (Nakamura Tōkichi / Itoh Kyuemon) along the Omotesandō. An early dinner (~16:30) at Dragon Burger 南禅寺店 by Nanzen-ji — wagyu burgers with yuzu/shiso/wasabi (~¥1,800) — before the Kōdai-ji light-up finale. (Still fancy Pontochō? It’s central and open late if you’d rather a later dinner.)
D5 Wed 18 Nov

Fushimi Inari, Kibune & Kurama

08:00 breakfast · 09:00 Fushimi Inari · 11:30 Enkō-ji + Ichijōji ramen · 13:30 Kurama-dera · 14:45 hike to Kibune · after dark, lit tunnel home

Flipped to chase the light-up. Start at Fushimi Inari early — two JR stops from the hotel and magical before the crowds — then ride north into the mountains, hike Kibune → Kurama, soak at Kurama Onsen while the light fades, and ride the Eizan Kirara home through the illuminated Momiji Tunnel after dark. Reversing the day means you keep the onsen and the lit tunnel.

  1. Fushimi Inari · from ~09:00 — breakfast, then leave ~8:30; two JR stops to Inari. Climb the torii tunnels (the Yotsutsuji viewpoint ~30 min up is enough). Go on the early side today — it’s a full day.
  2. Enkō-ji · ~11:30 — Keihan to Demachiyanagi, Eizan to Ichijōji: the reserved autumn viewing at Enkō-ji, a hillside maple garden with a famous bird’s-eye view over the grounds (~1 h). Book the timed slot — autumn reservations open Oct 15 at enkouji.jp (~¥1,500, last entry 16:30). Shisen-dō is a 2-min walk if you have time.
  3. Ichijōji ramen lunch · ~12:45 — Ichijōji is Kyoto’s ramen district, so grab a bowl before heading up.
  4. Up to Kurama · ~13:30 — back on the Eizan to the Kurama terminus (through the Momiji Tunnel in daylight on the way up).
  5. Kurama-dera · 14:00 — ride the little cable car (¥200) up to skip the first climb, then the mountainside main hall and valley views; the trail starts behind it.
  6. Over the ridge, down to Kibune · from ~14:45 — a short climb to the ridge (towering cedars and the Tree-Root Path), then a long, easy descent into Kibune and Kifune Shrine. Running behind? Skip the hike and train between Kurama and Kibuneguchi — you’ll still make the tunnel.
  7. Momiji Tunnel light-up · after dark — down to Kibuneguchi, then ride the Kirara home through the lit tunnel: lit ~16:50 to about 21:00, cabin lights off, the train slowed. It runs late, so no rush — ride once it’s properly dark. Walk-on local train, busy at peak; grab window seats.
Notes
Access via Demachiyanagi (Keihan → Eizan). With Enkō-ji you now make three Eizan hops (~¥1,050), so the ¥1,200 one-day pass is about break-even — worth it here for the freedom to hop on and off. Cable car ¥200 · Kurama-dera ¥500. Higher than the city, so mid-Nov should be near peak. The Momiji Tunnel light-up runs dusk–~21:00 in the autumn window — recent years ran early/mid-Nov through ~Nov 24, so the 18th is very likely covered, but confirm the 2026 dates at eizandensha.co.jp (posted from September). Keeping it gentle? Skip the ridge hike and take the train between Kibuneguchi and Kurama. Roots get slippery after rain; last bus down from Kibune ~6 pm. Want the onsen after all? Kurama Onsen (~¥2,000) is by Kurama station, at the start of the day.
Eat
At Inari, Nezameya by the station for kitsune udon and inari-zushi once it opens. Up the mountain, botan-nabe (wild-boar hotpot) is the Kurama–Kibune specialty; Yoshuji near Kurama Station does mountain-vegetable and sanshō dishes; Kibune’s riverside inns serve kaiseki lunches.
D6 Thu 19 Nov

Arashiyama, gorge & Takao

08:45 Torokko Saga · 09:02 gorge train · 10:00 Hozugawa boat · 12:00 Arashiyama lunch · 14:00 Takao: Jingo-ji → Saimyō-ji → Kōzan-ji · 18:00 Pokémon Center + dinner

The scenic heart of the Kyoto days — down a river gorge by rail, then by boat, capped with the trip's best-timed foliage valley. Skipping the bamboo grove, it runs point to point.

  1. Gion → Torokko Saga — JR Sagano Line via Kyoto Station to Saga-Arashiyama (~40 min); Torokko Saga is beside it. Arrive by 8:45.
  2. Sagano Torokko · 09:02 — the open-air scenic train up the Hozugawa gorge to Torokko Kameoka (~25 min). Book a month ahead; Thursday is a guaranteed running day.
  3. Shuttle to the pier — timed bus from Torokko Kameoka to the boat dock (~15 min, ¥350–500).
  4. Hozugawa river boat · 10:00 — float back down the ravine to Arashiyama (~2 h), landing at Togetsukyō around noon. No reservation needed.
  5. Lunch in Arashiyama — by the bridge; slot in a quick Tenryū-ji garden loop if you want more maples.
  6. Arashiyama → Takao — Randen tram to Uzumasa-Tenjingawa, then City Bus #8 (~40 min); in peak season a seasonal Kyoto Bus #90 runs direct. Be ready to stand.
  7. Takao · 14:00 — Jingo-ji first, then up the valley. From the Takao stop, climb the ~400 maple-lined steps to Jingo-ji (the kawarake disc-throwing lookout over the Kiyotaki gorge) — it closes at 16:00 and is the steepest, so do it first while fresh. Then walk north to Saimyō-ji (the red Shigetsu bridge) and World-Heritage Kōzan-ji (the “first manga” scrolls), both open to 17:00. Bus back from Toganoo. 2.5–3 h, brisk.
  8. Back downtown · dinner — bus/train to Shijō; the Pokémon Center Kyoto (SUINA Muromachi, Shijō-Karasuma) is right by Nishiki — pop in first since it closes 20:00, then dinner around Nishiki or Pontochō. (Alternatively, Jingo-ji runs a special evening opening some years, ~Nov 5–19 ~17:00–19:00 — if it’s on for the 19th, skip the Pokémon run and stay out at Takao: Saimyō-ji and Kōzan-ji first, then Jingo-ji after dark.)
Pokémon
Kyoto Pokémon Center — SUINA Muromachi 2F, Shijo, open to ~20:00. Takao fills this day, so swing by after dinner if you have energy — or catch it on the D4 or D5 evening, since it is central.
Eat
Coffee on tatami at Söt Coffee near Gion with their basque cheesecake; snacks along the Arashiyama approach. Dinner downtown around Pontochō or Nishiki Market.

Kanazawa

Days 7–8 · foliage peak
D7 Fri 20 Nov

Garden city

08:30 check out · 09:00 Thunderbird · 11:00 Kanazawa · 11:30 Ōmichō lunch · 12:45 castle → Kenroku-en · 15:45 21st Century Museum · 17:30 Forus: Pokémon Center, shopping + dinner

Check out of Kyoto and move to Kanazawa (~2 h with one transfer), then one walking line through the centre: market, castle, garden and the art museum across the road, then back to the station for a long evening of shopping.

  1. Kyoto → Kanazawa · depart 08:30 — the hotel is a 2-min walk from Kyoto Station, so no cross-city hop with luggage. Thunderbird to Tsuruga (~45 min), then a cross-platform transfer to the Hokuriku Shinkansen (~40 min). A 09:00 departure lands you at 11:00. Reserve both legs; keep bags light for the change.
  2. Drop bags · 11:00 — hotel near Kanazawa Station, or the station lockers.
  3. Ōmichō Market · lunch 11:30 — Kanazawa’s kitchen: kaisen-don, crab, sushi. Bus or a ~15-min walk from the station.
  4. Kanazawa Castle Park · 12:45 — ~10 min walk from the market to the Kuroman / Ōte gate; turrets, gates, grounds. Walk through and out the Ishikawa-mon gate. ~45 min.
  5. Kenroku-en · 13:30 — straight across the bridge from Ishikawa-mon. One of Japan’s three great gardens at its autumn best, the yukitsuri rope cones just going up. Leave by the Mayumizaka gate. 1.5–2 h.
  6. 21st Century Museum · 15:45 — just across the road from the Mayumizaka gate. Open to 20:00 on Fridays, so there’s no rush. On arrival, scan the QR at the entrance for the paid Swimming Pool chamber’s same-day waiting list. If it’s full, looking down into the pool from above is free.
  7. Forus · Pokémon Center & shopping · 17:30 — bus back to the station (~10 min). Pokémon Center Kanazawa is on Kanazawa Forus 5F (open to 20:00), a minute from your hotel’s East exit; the rest of the mall is fashion and lifestyle shops. For souvenirs — gold-leaf goods, Kutani ware, Kaga sweets — try the Hyakubangai shops inside the station. Dinner on Forus 6F around 19:00, or after the shops close.
Eat
Kaisendon at Omicho Ichiba Sushi in the market. Dinner by the station — Forus’s 6F “Kuugo” food floor (~20 restaurants), right above the Pokémon Center.
D8 Sat 21 Nov

Teahouses & samurai lanes

09:00 Higashi Chaya · 10:30 Kazuemachi · 11:45 Katamachi lunch · 13:00 Nagamachi · 14:30 Myōryū-ji · 15:30 Nishi Chaya · 18:00 Kenroku-en light-up

A slow day in town, with no trains: east to west through the old districts, then back to the centre for Kenroku-en lit up. Higashi Chaya goes first because it’s the holiday weekend’s busiest street by late morning. A breather before the two big bus days into the Alps.

  1. Higashi Chaya · 09:00 — bus from the station (~10 min). On the holiday Saturday, the teahouse lanes are only quiet before ~10:00; shops open from ~9:30–10:00. Step into a preserved teahouse such as Shima, then gold-leaf soft serve at Hakuichi.
  2. Kazuemachi · 10:30 — over the Asano river (~5 min walk): a smaller teahouse lane along the water, with maples on the bank. ~30 min.
  3. Lunch · 11:45 — bus to Kōrinbō / Katamachi (~10 min).
  4. Nagamachi · 13:00 — ~5 min walk from Kōrinbō: the earthen-walled samurai lanes and canals. Step into Nomura-ke, a samurai house with a small jewel of a garden. ~1 h.
  5. Myōryū-ji (Ninja Temple) · 14:30 — ~20 min walk south over the Sai river to Teramachi, or a short bus. A temple full of trap doors, hidden stairs and escape routes; guided tour only, phone booking required (¥1,200, tours every 30 min, 9:00–16:00). Tours are in Japanese with a printed English guide.
  6. Nishi Chaya · 15:30 — 5 min from the temple: a short, quieter teahouse street. Tea and wagashi, then a break — back to the hotel or a café — before the evening.
  7. Kenroku-en & castle light-up · 18:00 — the garden and Kanazawa Castle Park lit after dark, free, 18:00–20:45 (gates close 21:00). Confirmed for Sat 21 Nov 2026, one of seven weekend nights. Kenroku-en by daylight on D7, by lantern light tonight.
Notes
21 Nov opens the 23 Nov holiday weekend, so Higashi Chaya and Kenroku-en will be busy — start early. Get around on foot and Hokutetsu city buses; every hop is 5–20 min.
Eat
Cafe Colere, a book-lined café in Higashi Chaya, and Hakuichi for the gold-leaf soft serve. Lunch around Kōrinbō or Katamachi; tea and wagashi in Nishi Chaya. After the light-up, Kanazawa oden at a local counter, or a Katamachi izakaya.

Takayama

Days 9–10 · bus & rail base
D9 Sun 22 Nov

Bus to Shirakawa-gō & Takayama

08:10 bus from Kanazawa · 09:25 Shirakawa-gō · 09:45 Shiroyama view · 12:00 lunch · 13:15 bus · 14:05 Takayama · 14:45 Jinya · 15:30 Sanmachi · 18:30 Hida beef

Over the mountains by bus — a morning in the thatched village of Shirakawa-gō, then on to your Takayama base for two nights. It’s the holiday Sunday, so take the first bus and be in the village before the tour coaches. Skipping Gokayama, a quieter version of the same thing.

  1. Check out · 07:40 — breakfast early, then cross through Kanazawa Station from the East exit to the West exit bus terminal, stop #4 (~10 min with bags). Be at the stop by 08:00.
  2. Bus to Shirakawa-gō · 08:10 — the reserved Nohi / Hokutetsu highway bus (¥2,800), arriving ~09:25 at the Shirakawa-gō Bus Terminal. This is the first bus, ahead of the holiday-Sunday tour coaches. Big cases go in the shared trunk; space is limited.
  3. Stash bags · 09:30 — coin lockers at the bus terminal (¥500 small/medium, ¥1,000 large, 08:00–17:00), or the staffed luggage counter next door if they’re full. Pick up a village map at the information desk.
  4. Shiroyama viewpoint · 09:45 — the postcard view down over the thatched roofs. Do it first, before the crowds and while the light is soft: walk up the path at the north end of the village (~15–20 min, steep), or take the shuttle from in front of Wada House (¥200, every ~20 min, from ~10:00). Walk back down through the village.
  5. Village walk · 10:45 — south through Ogimachi along the rice paddies and canals. Go inside one gasshō house: Wada House is the largest, and Kanda House lets you climb into the roof frame. Then Myōzen-ji temple and the Shirakawa Hachiman shrine.
  6. Lunch · 12:00 — soba, Hida-beef croquettes and skewers, and gohei-mochi (grilled rice on a stick with miso) from the stalls along the main street. Finish by crossing the Deai-bashi suspension bridge for the river view, then collect bags by 13:00.
  7. Bus to Takayama · 13:15 — Shirakawa-gō → Takayama Nohi Bus Center (¥2,800), arriving ~14:05. Book this leg too; buses back up on holiday weekends, so expect some delay.
  8. Check in · 14:15 — HOTEL AMANEK is right across from the bus center. Drop bags for two nights.
  9. Takayama Jinya · 14:45 — ~10 min walk east: the only surviving Edo-era government office, with tatami halls and the rice storehouse. In November it closes at 16:30 (last entry 16:00), so it goes first. ~45 min.
  10. Sanmachi old town · 15:30 — cross the red Nakabashi bridge into the dark-wood merchant lanes. Shops and sake breweries (look for the cedar balls, sugidama, over the door) close around 17:00, so taste sake and pick up snacks now. Your D11 morning is for the markets, so this is your main Sanmachi visit.
  11. Dusk walk · 16:30 — optional: up to the temples of Higashiyama or Shiroyama Park, 10 min from Sanmachi, for foliage before sunset (~16:40). Otherwise rest at the hotel.
  12. Hida beef dinner · 18:30 — in the old town, ~8 min from the hotel.
  13. Evening — you’re right, Takayama is quiet at night: Sanmachi shuts by ~17:00 and the town is mostly dinner and a few izakaya. Walk the empty, lantern-lit lanes after dinner — the most atmospheric they get — then the hotel’s rooftop onsen.
Notes
Book two tickets on Japan Bus Online: Kanazawa 08:10 → Shirakawa-gō, and Shirakawa-gō 13:15 → Takayama. Backup buses to Takayama leave at 13:30 and 14:00. Shirakawa-gō is up in the mountains, so it can be near freezing — wear layers and shoes with grip. Bring cash for lockers and stalls.
Eat
Street food at Shirakawa-gō: soba, Hida-beef croquettes, gohei-mochi. Dinner: Hida beef at Kitchen Hida (reserve) or casual Hidatakayama Meat in the old town.
D10 Mon 23 Nov

Shinhotaka Ropeway (Okuhida)

07:40 Nohi bus · 09:30 ropeway up · 10:15 summit deck 2,156 m · 12:00 down · 12:55 bus · 13:30 Hirayu onsen · ~15:40 bus back · 18:30 dinner

Trade the old town for the high Alps — Japan’s only double-decker gondola into the Northern Alps, topping out over the snow-dusted Hida peaks, then a long soak in Hirayu’s outdoor baths on the way home. Early start: the summit is usually clearest in the morning.

  1. Ticket · 07:20 — at the Nohi Bus Center ticket office across from the hotel, buy the Okuhida Marugoto Value Ticket (¥7,700): a 2-day Takayama–Shinhotaka bus pass plus the ropeway round trip. Separately it’s ¥8,200 (bus ¥2,200 each way + ropeway ¥3,800), and the pass lets you hop off at Hirayu Onsen on the way back. No refunds, so check the ropeway is running first (status on shinhotaka-ropeway.jp).
  2. Bus to Shinhotaka · 07:40 — the Shinhotaka-bound Nohi bus via Hirayu Onsen, arriving at the Shinhotaka Ropeway stop at ~09:15 (~1 h 35). Buses don’t take reservations and it’s the holiday Monday, so queue early. The 08:40 bus (arriving ~10:15) is your backup.
  3. Up the ropeway · 09:30 — No. 1 Ropeway (every 30 min, at :00 and :30) to Nabedaira-kōgen, a short walk across to Shirakaba-daira, then the double-decker No. 2 Ropeway (at :15 and :45). ~25 min to the top. Stand by the window on the No. 2 gondola’s upper deck.
  4. Summit deck · 10:15 — Nishi-Hotakaguchi (2,156 m): the rooftop observation deck over the Hotaka and Yarigatake peaks. Mornings are usually clearest, which is why this goes first. It’s often below freezing with snow underfoot by late November. Coffee and a snack in the station café. ~1–1.5 h.
  5. Down · 12:00 — ropeway back to Shinhotaka Onsen; on the way, stop at Nabedaira-kōgen for lunch and the shops if you like. There’s a free footbath by the bottom station.
  6. Bus to Hirayu Onsen · 12:55 — ~35 min down the valley; hop off at the Hirayu bus terminal.
  7. Hirayu no Mori · 13:30 — ~5 min walk: a big day-bath complex with outdoor pools in the forest (¥600–700; open 10:00–21:00; bring or buy a towel). Soak, then a late snack in its rest area. ~2 h.
  8. Bus back to Takayama · ~15:40 — from Hirayu bus terminal (~1 h), in Takayama by ~16:40. Buses run roughly hourly; take a later one if you’re still soaking, but 16:55 is the last bus out of the ropeway, so don’t leave it later than that one.
  9. Dinner · 18:30 — Hida beef back in town, then the hotel’s rooftop onsen if you’re not soaked out.
Watch
The ropeway shuts for maintenance 24–27 Nov, so the 23rd is the last running day — no rescheduling, and it also closes in bad weather. It is a holiday Monday too, so go early; buses take no reservations. Pack your warmest layers, gloves and grippy shoes for the top. Wet-weather backup, all in Takayama: Hida Folk Village (open-air thatched-house museum) and the Festival Floats Exhibition Hall (indoors).
Eat
Hida-beef buns and fresh bread at the ropeway stations, or lunch at Nabedaira; a snack at Hirayu no Mori. Dinner: Hida beef back in Takayama at Kitchen Hida or Hidatakayama Meat.

Kōrankei + Obara

A double bill of autumn colour, both in Toyota City. The two spots are awkward to link by public transport in a day, so an English-guided one-day bus tour from Nagoya is the low-stress pick; DIY is Meitetsu to Toyota-shi, then the Oiden bus.

Before you go

Foliage timing

  • Minoh (Osaka): maples peak mid-to-late Nov — a great foliage half-day.
  • Kyoto & Uji: mid-to-late Nov is prime, at or near peak.
  • Kanazawa: Kenroku-en colours mid-to-late Nov. Good timing.
  • Takayama and Shirakawa-gō: higher and earlier, so possibly just past peak by the 22nd — the villages are still lovely.
  • Kōrankei and Obara: late Nov is peak, the foliage highlight of the trip, paired with Obara's rare autumn shikizakura.
  • Kōrankei & Obara on D12 are the foliage peak of the trip. Check a kōyō forecast a few weeks out and be ready to shuffle a day if the front runs early or late.

Pokémon Centers

  • Osaka · Umeda — Daimaru Umeda 13F (LUCUA 1100), the city’s bigger store, with Nintendo Osaka on the same floor. On D2, back from Minoh.
  • Osaka DX · Shinsaibashi — Daimaru Shinsaibashi 9F. Smaller, but the Pokémon Café is next door, reservation-only.
  • Nagoya · Sakae — PARCO East 2F. Handy on D13; weekends may need timed entry.
  • Kyoto · SUINA Muromachi 2F (Shijō-Karasuma, to 20:00) — best on D6, when you’re downtown for dinner anyway; pop in before you eat.
  • Kanazawa · Forus 5F — one minute from the station and your hotel, open to 20:00. On D7, at the end of the day.

The Hida leg by bus and rail (Days 9–11)

  • D9 Kanazawa → Shirakawa-gō → Takayama — Nohi / Hokutetsu highway bus (~1 h 15 + ~50 min). Reserve ahead; it fills over the holiday weekend.
  • D10 Takayama → Shinhotaka Ropeway — Nohi Bus from the Nohi Bus Center (~1 h 35, no reservation, roughly hourly); buy the Okuhida Marugoto ticket there. Last bus from the ropeway 16:55.
  • D11 Takayama → Nagoya — Limited Express Hida (~2.5 h) down the gorge; reserved window seat. Inuyama is a Meitetsu hop from Nagoya.
  • Luggage — reserve early for the space; on the Tōkaidō Shinkansen book an oversized-baggage seat if a case tops 160 cm total.
  • Pass — the Takayama–Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass covers most of this loop; price it against singles.
Heads-up

Holiday weekend

  • Mon 23 Nov is a public holiday (Labour Thanksgiving), so 21–23 Nov is a three-day weekend.
  • It overlaps Kanazawa, Shirakawa-gō and Takayama (D8–D10) — expect heavy crowds, full transport, and busy buses and ropeway. Kōrankei falls safely after, on Wed 25.
  • Book buses and reserved-seat trains well ahead, and start early at Shirakawa-gō.

Transport passes

  • The Takayama–Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass fits this loop unusually well — price it against point-to-point tickets.
  • An IC card (ICOCA, Suica or mobile) for local trains, subways and buses.
  • The nationwide JR Pass is probably not worth it for a route this compact.
